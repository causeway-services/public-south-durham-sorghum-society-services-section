# Introducing the South Durham Sorghum Society

Published on February 12, 2023 by emsenn

The South Durham Sorghum Society is a group of folk in the south part of
Durham, North Carolina, who grow and use sorghum.

## What's sorghum?

It's botanical name is *Sorghum bicolor*. It looks a lot like corn when its
growing, except with a big puff of seeds on top, instead of cobs along the
side.

## What's it used for?

The seeds can be used like a grain, the stalks can be used to make molasses
and - this is cool - the strands the seeds grow on are what brooms are
traditionally made from! (Another name for sorghum is "broom corn".)

## Why grow it?

Well, it can be used for lots, it grows pretty easily, and while it isn't that
popular these days, it's been grown here since the early days of colonial
settlement, which means it grows wild around here, all around I-40 and other
highways. That can help us create a grex, a strain of the plant that will have
strong diverse genetics, serving as a good base for cultivating variants that
have the qualities we want (good molasses for some, good grain for others...).

The South Durham Sorghum Society is planning to do two seed swaps this year -
one on March 12th, and another on November 12th. In addition to sharing
cultivated variants, we also ask participants to contribute a portion to our
grexing mix, which will also include seed gotten from elsewhere around the
world.

This first event, we'll be giving out a mix of sorghum seeds to anyone that
wants them, and will be happy to answer any questions folk might have about
how to succeed in growing it, with their particular garden situation.

We're also planning to organize a few events to talk about things like
processing sorghum grain and making molasses, in the future.

## How do I join?

You can just show up to any of our events, but if you want to help organize
those events, we do that on OpenCollective so message us there!

If you want to help us cover the expense of seeds & printing, you can
contribute here.

Our first event is going to be a Spring Seed Swap, on March 12th at Pineywood
Park. Come learn a bit about how to grow sorghum, and pick up some free seeds
to get yourself started!
